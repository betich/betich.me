At graduation ceremonies, one evident problem is that if you are trying to find the graduate to congratulate them, finding them will always be a hassle. Thousands of people are crammed in the faculty with limited space and you will mostly have to rely on texting or calling the graduate to ask them where they are.

This poses a problem - locating where the graduate is takes a lot of communication, especially for people not familiar to the faculty. And graduates that day will have to answer to so many calls and texts that it may be overwhelming for them. Messages and calls often get lost.

So the night before the event, I built a website that hopes to fix aspects of this problem: **The Bundit Tracker**.

The graduate's phone broadcasts their location and posts updates on where they are heading, the followers see where to go. All in one link.

## Feature 1:  Compass

The main screen is a compass. It tells you how far away the graduate is and which way you have to walk to get to them.

## Feature 2 : Map

The second tab is a plain map with both pins on it and the line between them, so you can see where you are *relative to* the graduate rather than just how far.

The compass is better when you're close and moving. The map is better when you're deciding which building to start walking towards.

## Feature 3: Updates

The third tab is a feed. The graduate can post a photo and a line of text, where they are, where they're heading next, what they're doing, and everyone following will get the context of what they are doing at that moment.

I hoped that this feature will replace phone calls - updates should communicate sufficiently on what the graduate is doing and where they are. In cases where the phone's cellular signal is bad, causing the GPS feature to be inaccurate, this also provides a failsafe for locating them.

There's a live viewer count on the admin screen too, which I added as a debugging aid and kept because it's a nice feeling. **XX people are waiting for you**.

## When GPS isn't enough - Pinpoint accuracy

The admin can also pin their exact location on a building-level map under a crosshair and just *say* where they are. That point then overrides GPS until they tap back.

## Limitations

**Phone GPS is not as good as the number suggests.** Two consumer fixes disagree by metres even when the devices are touching, and both under-report their own error.   
  
**Mobile networks fall over in crowds.** A graduation rehearsal puts thousands of people with thousands of phones into a few hundred metres, and the cell towers there were not built for that.  

Both cases can be mitigated by pin-pointing their exact location and posting updates, but not solvable directly.
